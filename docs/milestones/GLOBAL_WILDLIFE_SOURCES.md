# Next milestone: worldwide wildlife updates

Research checked 7 November 2026 UTC. This is the next product milestone: expand beyond NZ towards a globe receiving diverse species updates. Broad geographic activity and separate identified-animal streams are both required. An acoustic station, a reported sighting and a satellite-tagged animal remain distinct. Worldwide coverage is a direction, not a claim that every region currently supplies data.

### WHOI Ocean City buoy: structured source verified, stale

At **04:19:50 UTC**, anonymous [MARACOOS ERDDAP metadata](https://erddap.maracoos.org/erddap/info/Ocean_City_buoy_dmon/index.json) returned HTTP 200, 9,385 bytes. Coverage ends **2026-07-18T03:59:19Z**; modification date is July 19. This endpoint is stale on November 7 despite its real-time title. At **04:19:52**, one ten-hour time-constrained JSON request returned 14 records (1,615 bytes), **00:44:19–03:59:19 July 18**, 15 minutes apart. Both checks used 20-second timeouts and 1 MiB response limits.

Species columns `sei`, `fin`, `right`, `humpback`: **1 present, 2 possible, 0 otherwise**. All sampled values were zero: no positive animal events in this sample. `platform=mdoc`, position near 38.3026 N, 74.6444 W: buoy identity/location, no animal ID. Dataset permission explicitly allows free use and redistribution, with accuracy/liability limitations and exclusion of legal use; no separate mandatory acknowledgement field was present.

The [WHOI mission page](https://dcs.whoi.edu/mdoc2607/mdoc2607_mdoc.shtml) confirms analyst-reviewed **15-minute summary periods**, not individual call instants or whale abundance. Treat `time` as observation-summary time; interval-boundary convention needs confirmation before declaring exact start/end bounds. Metadata modification is not per-record upload time. Preserve possible detections and negatives separately; never generate positive events from zero values. Attribute WHOI/UMCES and MARACOOS and retain the mission's funder/NOAA acknowledgements with exports; the page identifies NOAA award NA23NOS4190254 but does not state an additional API-use acknowledgement requirement. First fixture gates: 0/1/2/null/unknown codes, interval precision, stale latest time, duplicate/review corrections and station identity. Current-live qualification remains open for other WHOI datasets.

### MARACOOS DMON catalog cross-check

At **04:22:11 UTC**, the anonymous `allDatasets` query for detection/DMON titles returned **29 DMON datasets**, HTTP 200, 4,381 bytes. Coverage spans US Atlantic/Canadian waters, Chukchi Sea and California. No catalog `maxTime` was within the preceding seven days. The newest plausible date was **Gulf_of_Maine_Fall_Slocum_glider_dmon: 2026-10-23T17:44:23Z** (about 14 days old), followed by Chukchi Sea October 2; other plausible endpoints were older.

**Seven catalog end dates anomalously fall in 1995**, including New York Bight NW/SE buoys, Cape Charles, Savannah and Laurentian Channel A/B. A bounded New York NW metadata check at **04:22:25** confirmed `time_coverage_end=1995-11-06T21:48:44Z`, numeric epoch range and `seconds since 1970`, alongside `date_modified=2026-11-01` and a 2026 mission link. This is a timestamp-integrity problem requiring provider clarification, not evidence that the physical program stopped in 1995. Do not guess an epoch correction or use file modification as observation time.

One filtered [Gulf of Maine sample endpoint](https://erddap.maracoos.org/erddap/tabledap/Gulf_of_Maine_Fall_Slocum_glider_dmon.html) at **04:22:26** returned **15 fin-positive rows**, HTTP 200, 1,386 bytes, for October 22–24 bounds: observations **October 22 03:45:35–October 23 16:30:35 UTC**, `fin=1`, other three species columns zero, `platform=we15`, near 44.02–44.06 N / 67.93–67.97 W. These are real positive acoustic summaries at robot positions; they are neither current November updates nor whale tracks. All three checks used 20-second/1 MiB limits.

Conclusion: structured, reusable WHOI data extends beyond the retired/stale Ocean City snapshot and contains verified positive whale detections. **This catalog does not currently qualify a trustworthy fresh live stream**: either plausible observations are at least two weeks old or advertised time metadata are inconsistent. This finding concerns the checked MARACOOS mirror, not all WHOI deployments. Stop qualification here pending a documented current endpoint or corrected metadata; retain these as history candidates and add epoch/date-integrity regression gates.

### Bounded sighting sample

At **04:11:43 UTC**, one anonymous [iNaturalist query](https://api.inaturalist.org/v1/observations?taxon_id=1&license=cc0,cc-by&geoprivacy=open&taxon_geoprivacy=open&order_by=created_at&order=desc&per_page=3) returned HTTP 200, three records, 60,314 bytes. Record **413281089** was observed **October 27, 15:00:07 +11:00**, created **November 7, 15:11:37 +11:00**; record **413281065** was observed **November 7, 13:35:57 +10:00**, created **14:11:20 +10:00**. Both were CC-BY and publicly unobscured. Thus a newly uploaded sighting can be eleven days old. Sample access proves neither complete coverage nor stable ordering during index updates.

Record observation time, upload/update time, first seen and ingest time independently. A date-only observation remains date precision; no invented midnight instant. [Privacy guidance](https://help.inaturalist.org/en/support/solutions/articles/151000169938) describes randomized obscured coordinates: never infer precise locations, join observer histories to undo obscuring, or animate obscured points as exact fixes. First slice should exclude obscured/private records and unknown licence, retain positional accuracy, and recheck privacy/licence changes. [Licence filtering](https://www.inaturalist.org/pages/search%2Burls) applies to observations separately from media.

## First implementation slice and finite operating policy

1. Generalize the existing BirdWeather region selection into world regions with independent status/cursors and arrival baselines. Rotate **Oceania, Asia, Africa, Europe, North America, South America** under one shared provider request budget. Start with representative documented bounds; handle antimeridian splits and overlaps by event ID. Sparse regions must receive their turn even when busy regions saturate. Show observed regional availability honestly.
2. Preserve four total BirdWeather requests per minute initially, 250 nodes/page and 4 MiB/response. Allocate one request to each scheduled region before spending remaining requests on continuation; carry coverage state and explicitly report capped windows. Revisit each region within the ten-minute rolling lookback or flag a possible gap. This is a proposed conservative client budget, not a published provider quota.
3. Qualify one non-bird current marine acoustic source and one released identified-animal dataset independently. Other WHOI datasets are the first current-source investigation; Ocean City and OTN are concrete history candidates. Require access, actual sample timestamps, provenance and reuse evidence. If unavailable, keep it visibly unqualified and continue the shortlist; do not replace the identity requirement with generic sightings.
4. If enabled, sample iNaturalist across regions and taxa (mammals, reptiles, amphibians, fish, invertebrates and birds) every 15 minutes, at most four requests per cycle, up to 200 observations/page. Keep retrieval neutral; any musical region/taxon balancing occurs downstream with omission counts and preserved raw provenance. Initial query/filter semantics must be checked against the [current API](https://api.inaturalist.org/v1/docs/), including update/correction and deletion handling. Never assume deprecated endpoint behavior.
5. All connectors run off playback/UI threads. One in-flight request/provider; timeout, byte/record limits, cancellation and bounded queues. Respect `Retry-After`; exponential backoff with jitter for 429/503/network failures, capped at one hour, no parallel retries or IP rotation. Persist checkpoints only after durable validated ingestion. A failed region cannot suppress healthy regions. iNaturalist's [official guidance](https://www.inaturalist.org/pages/api%2Brecommended%2Bpractices) requests approximately one request/second and 10,000/day; this plan stays far below that. Other quotas remain provider-specific and unverified until documented.

### Provider-supported BirdWeather alternatives

The official schema documents detections filtered by bounding box, country, continent, species/classification and station, plus cursor paging. `newDetection` subscriptions support countries/continents/stations/species/classifications; geographic bounding-box subscription arguments were not verified. Connection transport, anonymous subscription access, quotas, reconnect and missed-event recovery remain untested. This is a later qualification path, not a promised drop-in fix.

The documented `period` argument is a relative duration. This review did **not** verify a fixed start/end history window or publication cursor for GraphQL detections. The station-authenticated REST API documents time bounds, but its station credentials and 100-record maximum do not establish anonymous global historical access. No further BirdWeather detection requests were made during this source review.

The alternative Wildlife Network API includes BirdWeather under a display-only agreement and directs redistribution to separate versioned open releases. Exclude this aggregator from fallback acquisition of BirdWeather detections. Track permission for local storage, map display, scene export and redistribution independently. The direct provider terms describe a licence granted to Scribe by contributors. [Wildlife Network developer guidance](https://wlnw.org/developers) · [BirdWeather terms](https://www.birdweather.com/terms-of-service)

## Acceptance gates

- Proposed first worldwide beta demonstration: record a 24-hour interval with real recent observations from at least three world regions and two independent providers, including a non-bird taxon. Publish counts by provider, region and taxon, observed-age distribution and gaps. Qualify each source against its own documented reporting cadence. Keep unobserved regions visibly quiet and treat further geographic expansion as continuing work.
- Reserve a share of the bounded globe and feed views for distinct active regions and source types, with counts for records omitted from the display. Keep the exact newest observation identifiable. Exercise one high-volume bird region alongside a quiet region and a sparse non-bird source.

- Disposable fixtures prove cross-region ID dedupe, separate baselines, sparse-region fairness, antimeridian handling, capped continuation, retry/cancel and quota accounting; restart/failure cannot fabricate completeness or replay baseline as live.
- Recorded source samples prove observation versus publication/ingest time, timezone/date precision, correction handling, stale uploads, public precision and record licence. Animal identity is namespaced and deployment-scoped; a tag, receiver, survey or observer is never promoted to an animal without evidence.
- Current, historical and reported-sighting streams remain separately filterable. History imports cannot pulse as current movement. Tracks require genuine identity and ordered fixes; acoustic detections cannot create animal tracks.
- Report accepted events, species, regions, source age, gaps and sampling/omission per stream. World coverage is assessed by actual events across distinct geographic regions over a recorded interval, not number of configured regions or animated dots.
- Before enabling each provider: zero-fee access established, rights/attribution recorded, sensitive-location policy enforced, one bounded live smoke against disposable state, then native regression checks. No owner DB fixtures, no interference with active collection.

Status: research and operating design only; the separate 250-node BirdWeather repair is implemented/tested. New world-region scheduler and new connectors are not implemented by this document. No claim of complete global coverage or newly verified current individual tracking is made.
