# Global and introduced-species sound-design research

Research date: 7 November 2026. Candidate work only; no production edits, imported samples, direct listening, signal measurements or owner listening approval. This file covers 21 requested named species/taxon candidates and one unresolved bat-order record. It complements the native-species brief; it is not an exhaustive world repertoire survey.

The linked official species accounts and their recorder-attributed archive references establish context and descriptive starting points. Most examples are from New Zealand; spotted dove examples are from Fiji/Indonesia, and legacy cabaret examples are European. Published descriptions support only the observations stated below. Every musical gesture, register choice, envelope and numeric control is a hypothesis. Attack/decay values are synthesizer controls, not measured animal timing.

No copyrighted audio was downloaded. Public playback links do not confer sampling or redistribution rights. [NZBO copyright policy](https://www.nzbirdsonline.org.nz/copyright) distinguishes personal access from reproduction and restricts commercial reuse and unauthorized metadata mining. The Sound Approach references have no reusable licence verified here. The JSON stores selected source identifiers and original context paraphrases; it must not become a bulk extraction or sample-collection manifest.

## Design implications

Do not treat the current three-partial struck engine, or any fixed set of character names, as the full design space. The useful reusable capabilities are continuous pitch/spectral contours, independent tonal and noisy envelopes, harmonic and formant-like shaping, bounded subnote patterns, variable phrase duration, labelled context variants, and register-aware orchestration. Taxonomic kinship is useful for related starting materials; audible distinction must be tested independently.

A family resemblance should survive transposition, and a species distinction should survive equal loudness. Contrast phrase structure as well as spectra: Turdus variation versus repetition; finch rolls versus buzzes versus cadences; goose swell versus mallard pulses; coot clonk versus swamphen nasal break; myna declaration versus starling montage. These are musical hypotheses, not claims that an event supplies behavioural/sex information.

Current-control notation below is tuning in semitones, attack in ms, decay in seconds, gain in dB. Allowed ranges: tuning −12…12, attack 2…100, decay 0.1…3, gain −60…0. All candidates need actual source listening followed by synthetic, level-matched evaluation.

## House sparrow — Passer domesticus

`birdweather:species:1`; Passeridae. exact species; retain provider identity.

**Documented:** The account distinguishes the ordinary social/song chirp from a rougher alarm. Selected references cover an individual in vegetation, a flock and chattering. [Source account / primary publication](https://www.nzbirdsonline.org.nz/species/house-sparrow).

**Coverage:** Chirp repetition is documented; cadence is unmeasured. The source calls the basic voice unmelodious; natural pitch range, spectral balance and envelope times remain unmeasured.

**Reference range (metadata reviewed; not listened):**

- [CbGD432kQLWR0fEVPnepig](https://www.nzbirdsonline.org.nz/assets/95597/1691644856-01-20-20unknown-20title-20-20unknown-20artist.mp3): individual shrub call; St Albans Christchurch; 1969-10-02; Les McPherson.
- [fAYZ3U-oQx23TYYoxPCl4A](https://www.nzbirdsonline.org.nz/assets/95597/1691644870-22-20-20house-20sparrow.mp3): flock social calls; Christchurch; 1971-06-02; Les McPherson.
- [EqBbZ_rZQyiHs7iZMAJjng](https://www.nzbirdsonline.org.nz/assets/95597/1691644895-nia-202409-20house-20sparrow.mp3): chattering; Waitaki Waters Otago; 2017-01-02; Nicholas Allen.

**Musical hypothesis:** Use a compact, woody, slightly nasal keyboard chip: two unequal pulses, tiny upward scoops and dry silence between clusters. Flock density becomes restrained overlap, not a continuous glitter wash.

**Gesture:** upper-mid; clipped attack; mildly rough harmonic body; uneven two/three-note cells; fast release.

**Related voices and distinction:** Keep shorter and drier than goldfinch; no long greenfinch buzz. Sparrow-like appearance of dunnock is not a family relationship.

**Initial current controls:** 3, 4, 0.24, -18. Extensions: band-limited noise transient, nasal spectral emphasis, bounded pulse clustering.

**Reference range (metadata reviewed; not listened):**

- [BtWdOo-eSLWLeGyxj6AjGw](https://www.nzbirdsonline.org.nz/assets/95597/1691641772-51-20-20malay-20spotted-20dove.mp3): tree-perching adults, Fiji; Adventist Cemetery Lami Suva Fiji; 1986-06-02; Les McPherson.

**Musical hypothesis:** Use a warm low sine/woodwind voice with three rounded swells. Each swell rises slightly then relaxes, and the final short syllable closes the pattern. Phrase space matters more than reverberation.

**Documented:** The account distinguishes female falling-intensity series of roughly six to eight quacks, quieter maternal communication and a softer male voice. Selected archive references include both sexes and nesting-female alarm. [Source account / primary publication](https://www.nzbirdsonline.org.nz/species/mallard).

**Coverage:** Sex-linked contrast and decreasing intensity are documented; formant frequencies, exact spacing and envelope times are unmeasured. The archive hybrid recording is excluded from exact-species evidence.

- [AFTcRD5RSXaRZgptPWzfPQ](https://www.nzbirdsonline.org.nz/assets/95597/1691646985-02-20-20track-202_22.mp3): quiet male and female calls; Mount Bruce National Wildlife Centre; 1979-08-02; Les McPherson.
- [T14w5uAnTpa7XYEBtvpB6g](https://www.nzbirdsonline.org.nz/assets/95597/1691646989-16-20-20track-2016_11.mp3): nesting-female alarm; Oreti gravel pits Otatara near Invercargill Southland; 1979-12-02; Les McPherson.

**Musical hypothesis:** Use a compact hollow bassoon-like pulse with gentle rasp. A female-inspired variant repeats with decreasing velocity; the male-inspired variant is softer and shorter. Keep explicit variant labels rather than inferring sex from an event.

**Gesture:** low-mid; quick soft-edged front; hollow nasal/rough tone; decaying pulse train; rounded short release.

**Related voices and distinction:** Anatidae sibling of goose; shorter pulse groups and declining accents give mallard its own musical shape.

**Initial current controls:** -6, 10, 0.48, -24. Extensions: formant-like spectral filtering, per-subnote gain contour, labelled sex/context variants.

**Reference range (metadata reviewed; not listened):**

**Initial current controls:** -3, 9, 0.7, -25. Extensions: resonant spectral peaks, controlled roughness/noise, context-specific two-part phrase.

`birdweather:species:913`; Fringillidae. legacy exact named taxon; IOC now places it within Acanthis flammea.

**Documented:** Primary recordists describe compact fast flight-call groups, commonly two or three units, sometimes interspersed with an upward-inflected social call. **Reference range (metadata reviewed; not listened):**

## Listening and design qualification still required

1. Play each linked source context and document what was actually heard, excluding background species. Record time-stamped notes and any uncertain identity, capture/edit artefacts or playback failure. Do not change the current zero-listened evidence label until this happens.
2. Where use is permitted, inspect sonograms to separate pitch contour, harmonic spacing, noisy components, note grouping, within-phrase amplitude and silence. Measurements need an explicit method; no such values are supplied by this review.
3. Render at least two synthesis approaches per main family: additive/FM or wavetable/reed for whistles, shaped excitation/resonators for hollow calls, and separate filtered-noise/transient layers for rough gestures. Use only generated excitation and lawful reference listening.
4. Compare individual sounds and dense mixed arrangements at matched perceived loudness. Evaluate audibility, pleasing repetition, fatigue, similarity/confusion and preservation of phrase identity at different host pitches and event densities.
5. Ask for owner listening decisions on concrete renders. Do not mark researched, implemented, playable and listening-approved as interchangeable.

## Primary research on timbre identity, related voices and orchestration

This is an independent perceptual research layer beyond Field engineering guidance. It supports experiments, not a predetermined number of oscillator characters. A taxonomic family, an acoustic-gesture family, a synthesis method and an arrangement role are separate dimensions; one should not silently stand in for another.

**McAdams, Winsberg, Donnadieu, De Soete and Krimphoff (1995).** Ratings of synthetic instrument timbres were modelled with common dimensions, sound-specific attributes and listener classes. Attack rise, spectral centre and spectral change helped explain the common dimensions; listeners weighted cues differently. [Primary paper: Perceptual scaling of synthesized musical timbres: Common dimensions, specificities, and latent subject classes](https://sites.music.mcgill.ca/orchestration/files/2014/10/McAdams_1995_PsycholRes.pdf).

**Design inference:** Use related starting bodies plus distinctive temporal/spectral gestures, with owner evaluation. A fixed brightness-only taxonomy or a fixed small palette cannot be justified by this study. **Limit:** Instrument stimuli and perceptual ratings do not establish animal taxonomy, natural vocal structure or a complete synthesis parameter set.

**Caclin, McAdams, Smith and Winsberg (2005).** Controlled synthetic-tone experiments supported attack time, spectral centre and spectrum fine structure as perceptually useful dimensions. Spectral flux was less consistently salient and depended on how many other dimensions varied. [Primary paper: Acoustic correlates of timbre space dimensions: a confirmatory study using synthetic tones](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16119366/).

**Design inference:** Make attack shape, spectral envelope and harmonic distribution independently controllable; audition spectral motion against competing changes instead of assuming it always creates an obvious identity. **Limit:** This is evidence about those stimuli, not a guarantee that a parameter separates all 32 species or that four controls suffice.

**Elhilali, Ma, Micheyl, Oxenham and Shamma (2009).** Human perceptual results showed that synchronous distant spectral components could group together even when the comparable successive components separated. The authors compared behavioural and ferret neural results and proposed a temporal-coherence model. [Primary paper: Temporal Coherence in the Perceptual Organization and Cortical Representation of Auditory Scenes](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2673083/).

**Design inference:** If independent species need to remain audible, test separate phrase accents, gaps and contour timing alongside register and timbre; identical quantized pulses may fuse. Use synchrony deliberately when a blended ensemble is wanted. **Limit:** The experiment does not prescribe arbitrary event-time changes or prove reliable identification in a dense animal arrangement.

**Fischer, Soden, Thoret, Montrey and McAdams (2021).** Three orchestral-excerpt experiments found greater segregation with timbral heterogeneity, and lower segregation after reducing timbral differences. Ratings of isolated-stream blend did not predict global segregation; several musical/acoustic cues contributed. [Primary paper: Instrument Timbre Enhances Perceptual Segregation in Orchestral Music](https://online.ucpress.edu/mp/article/38/5/473/117149/Instrument-Timbre-Enhances-Perceptual-Segregation).

**Design inference:** Judge presets in actual event arrangements as well as in isolation. Test phrase overlap, crossings and simultaneous attacks; a pleasant isolated voice may still disappear in context. **Limit:** The work used one/two-stream orchestral excerpts. It does not establish a maximum number of identifiable species or guarantee that more contrast always sounds better.

**Walker (2002).** Three experiments examined listeners’ scaling/polarity expectations for conceptual variables and auditory mappings, then tested matching auditory graphs to data. Appropriate mapping direction and amount depended on the concept. [Primary paper: Magnitude Estimation of Conceptual Data Dimensions for Use in Sonification](https://sonify.psych.gatech.edu/publications/pdfs/2002JEPA-Walker.pdf).

**Design inference:** Validate actual data mappings separately from artistic species timbre. Do not assume that louder means more certain, higher means further north, or a faster phrase means an individual moved faster. Label the chosen mapping and preserve source uncertainty. **Limit:** This supports testing mappings; it does not validate the specific metaphor proposals in this bank.

**Hermann (2008).** This methodological paper distinguishes approaches such as direct parameter mapping and model-based sonification, and discusses the relationship of information-bearing sound and artistic uses. [Primary paper: Taxonomy and definitions for sonification and auditory display](https://www.icad.org/Proceedings/2008/Hermann2008.pdf).

**Design inference:** Keep traceable data relationships distinct from musical arrangement choices and source-inspired metaphors. A white-shark location can trigger an expressly artistic sound without being presented as an animal vocalization. **Limit:** A proposed taxonomy is a design framework, not a perceptual validation or an implementation benchmark.

### A concrete evaluation method

1. Compare near neighbours first: blackbird/thrush, the four finch candidates, myna/starling, goose/mallard, coot/swamphen and pheasant/peafowl. Equalize musical pitch, perceived loudness and presentation duration where the question is timbral identity. Rate distinction and liking separately.
2. Reintroduce each voice’s own phrasing, then compare transpositions and short/long variants. Ask whether the related core remains recognizable and the gesture still distinguishes it. Do not force untrained listeners to identify a natural species from an abstract musical voice.
3. Use one captured event set for arrangement comparisons. Test sparse/dense passages, crossing parts, simultaneous onsets, gaps, region processing and reverb. Keep the underlying event provenance fixed and report compositional transformations explicitly.
4. Run small randomized sessions with repeated examples and an unsure option. A confusion matrix or pairwise distinction ratings can reveal collisions; an equally important separate rating asks whether the sound is musically welcome over repeated use. No acceptance scores have been measured here.
5. Test data meaning separately: can a listener perceive the intended change in event count, location uncertainty or playback time without mistaking timbral changes for new species? Artistic mapping and factual interpretation need different success criteria.

A practical design ledger should therefore keep: source species/rank, documented context, proposed synthesis mechanism, perceptual body, distinctive phrase, arrangement role, controllable variation and audition status. It should allow several synthesis methods and several approved contexts for a species. No paper cited here establishes that all species must remain separately identifiable when sounding simultaneously, or that a fixed six-character implementation is the full instrument.
