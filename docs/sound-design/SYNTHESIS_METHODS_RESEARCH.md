# Synthesis-method research for musical species voices

7 November 2026. Extensive targeted primary-source review, not an exhaustive literature review. This research supports musical representations of exact species across varied documented calls. It does not propose sample reproduction, species-identification software, a six-character product ceiling, or a claim that one algorithm fits all animals. Field skills supplied relevant evidence/realtime discipline only; the synthesis scope is defined by the instrument and owner.

## Decision and evidence boundary

A small, dynamic additive candidate is justified for the immediate bank because it gives explicit frequency admission, deterministic state, direct control of spectral contrasts and straightforward peak bounds. A bounded phase/amplitude gesture changes the note through time. The experimental `air` character is a sparse diffuse multisine, not broadband noise; the `roughness` control is periodic AM, not biological roughness. These limitations are reasons to audition richer candidates, not to relabel the approximation.

Static spectra plus different envelopes are insufficient as a general design for these species. The exact-species briefs include long trills, separate call contexts, rising/slowing gestures, ringing versus coarse segments, and related species whose distinguishing material occurs within a phrase. This is an instrument-specific design inference from the species research. No reference animal audio was auditioned for those briefs; no source-derived oscillator settings, trained weights, spectra or recording assets enter this implementation.

The implementation selected after this review is specified separately in [VOICE_CHARACTER_CONTRACT.md](../milestones/VOICE_CHARACTER_CONTRACT.md). Numerical correctness, reproducible output, audible contrast, musical preference, source evocation, hardware timing and final preset approval are different gates. None implies the others.

## Comparative design space

The following costs are structural estimates, not product benchmarks. Every method still needs rate-specific bounds, onset/recall/reset semantics, controlled level matching and measured callback qualification.

| Method | Useful sound/control relation | Cost, persistence and state | Main rejection probe |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dynamic additive / harmonic synthesis | Independently moving partial amplitudes and frequencies; clean whistles, odd-rich tones, inharmonic ringing; explicit contour | O(partials) per voice/sample; persist authored spectral/control parameters, not oscillator phase; bounded oscillator bank is simple to cap | At matched pitch and loudness, can a small bank supply convincing contrast without a thin organ-like result? |
| Subtractive synthesis | Rich harmonic or noise excitation into moving filters; breath, grit, calls with shifting spectral envelope | Few oscillators plus filter sections; retain cutoff/Q/excitation shape and smoothing policy; filters have memory and stability/headroom obligations | Does moving filtering improve texture enough to justify alias-suppressed excitation and modulation-safe filter work? |
| Audio-rate FM / PM | Compact dynamic spectral expansion; metal, reed-like energy, growling or ringing changes; harmonicity follows frequency relations | A few oscillators can replace many partials; persist carrier/modulator ratios, indices and their independent envelopes; sideband budget and modulation-rate semantics are essential | Test high notes/max index against a converged high-rate reference; reject foldover or uncontrolled brightness/pitch coupling |
| Wavetable / spectral morphing | Efficient rich periodic shape; continuous movement between original authored spectra | Runtime lookup/interpolation and possibly table blending; immutable tables need content/version identity and safe lifetime; pitch-dependent band limits/tables | High-pitch sweep, table transitions and interpolation images; do not assume a large table alone removes aliasing |
| Modal resonators | Explicit resonant modes, material-like partial decay, excitation/body relation | O(modes); independent frequency/damping/excitation; persist mode design, not ringing state; require energy/decay/retirement bound | Mutes/reset, dense repeated excitation, mode interaction, and whether material response helps this voice |
| Digital waveguide / physical model | Coupled excitation and resonating body with coherent response to pressure/tension-like gestures | Delay buffers, interpolation, losses, reflection/nonlinear excitation; persist physical controls and model revision; solver/delay work bounded | Pitch changes, excitation stability, finite-state recovery and whether physical controls remain musically predictable |
| Formant / source-filter / FOF | Separate pitched/noisy excitation and spectral-envelope resonances; vowel-like calls, resonant hollows, multiple articulation types | A small resonator/filter bank or overlapping windowed impulses; persist source/envelope/centre/bandwidth controls; waveform/source changes can alias | Keep formants perceptually stable while pitch moves; test voiced/unvoiced switch and transient gain/stability |
| Granular / pulsar / microsound | Texture from synthetic small events, clouds, chatter density, clustered or rhythm-to-tone transitions | O(max simultaneous grains), not just average density; fixed pool and overlap/tail policy; persist grain distributions/window/seed, not audio chunks unless licensed | Distinguish useful texture from smeared attacks or meaningless randomness; cap density, subvoices and parameter bandwidth |
| Spectral / sinusoidal-plus-noise / transient models | Separate tonal trajectories, residual envelope and transient articulation; efficient abstract features from richer analysis | Offline analysis or windowed live transform; persist authored trajectories/filter envelopes and model revision; window/hop/latency and data rights explicit | Transient smear, phase discontinuity, changed-rate reconstruction and false claims of zero-window latency |
| Filtered stochastic noise plus gestures | True residual/breath/rasp component alongside tonal material; independent noise colour and onset/phrase envelopes | Seeded PRNG plus filters; usually few operations but filter norms/output statistics need proof; persist seed policy/filter parameters | Compare with sparse multisine at equal level: if noise texture materially helps, add it as a distinct bounded mechanism |
| Low-dimensional biomechanical / neural synthesis | Physical nonlinear control or learned latent control can express richer animal-source behaviours | Solver/training/data/weights and state identity; significantly different evidence and licensing burden; not automatically predictable or realtime | Can it generalise across actual requested species and musical range, with bounded runtime and controllable expressive gestures? |

These methods overlap. A source-filter model can use additive excitation, a formant can be approximated with FM, a granular oscillator can itself use FM, and an additive bank can render modes. The useful architecture boundary is authored musical behaviour plus bounded synthesis capabilities, not an assumption that each animal needs its own custom engine.

## Consequential findings from primary/authored sources

Chowning's original FM work makes spectral evolution a central control, using a time-varying modulation index to change bandwidth and showing harmonic and inharmonic outcomes. It is a perceptual synthesis technique rather than an anatomical model. The source supports dynamic spectra as an option; it does not prove FM superior for these species or establish a modern alias/CPU budget. [Chowning original article](https://aes.org/publications/elibrary/elibrary-page/?id=1954), [1977 authorised reprint at University of Victoria](https://web.uvic.ca/~aschloss/course_mat/MU307/MUS307_MATERIALS/Chowning_FM.pdf).

Smith describes additive synthesis as a sum of sinusoids with separately varying amplitudes and phase/frequency envelopes. This directly supports a compact bank when important components are relatively sparse. It does not establish that eight partials adequately express a raspy or noisy animal sound. [Additive synthesis section](https://www.dsprelated.com/freebooks/sasp/Additive_Synthesis_Early_Sinusoidal.html).

The sines-plus-noise approach adds a separately filtered stochastic component to the tonal model; the transient extension recognises that neither smoothly tracked peaks nor noise alone handles all abrupt events well. The present sparse `air` voice is deliberately weaker than that model. A true noise/transient candidate is a worthwhile next comparison for harsh calls and air-like textures. [Sines-plus-noise model](https://www.dsprelated.com/freebooks/sasp/Sines_Noise_Modeling.html), [transient extension](https://www.dsprelated.com/freebooks/sasp/Sines_Noise.html).

FM/PM need precise terminology. With phase `phi(t)=wc*t+I(t)*sin(wm*t)`, instantaneous frequency is the derivative, including both `I'(t)*sin(wm*t)` and `I(t)*wm*cos(wm*t)`. A varying-index phase-modulation implementation is therefore not automatically the same as simply integrating a sinusoidal frequency-deviation signal with the same named envelope. This derivative is independently derived here. Smith's FM discussion also shows compact formant-group approximation, a useful future vocal candidate. [FM synthesis and formant-group discussion](https://www.dsprelated.com/freebooks/sasp/Frequency_Modulation_FM_Synthesis.html).

For discontinuous classic waveforms, naive sampling is the problem; filtering a resulting digital saw afterwards does not undo foldover already created at the oscillator. Stilson and Smith compare additive truncation, wavetable interpolation and bandlimited primitive techniques, including the risk of spectral components popping when the generated harmonic count changes. This motivates a fixed admitted set over the entire new-note sweep. It does not make finite-envelope modulated output mathematically bandlimited. [Stilson and Smith, ICMC 1996](https://quod.lib.umich.edu/cgi/p/pod/dod-idx/alias-free-digital-synthesis-of-classic-analog-waveforms.pdf?c=icmc%3Bidno%3Dbbp2372.1996.101%3Bformat%3Dpdf).

Franck and Välimäki treat wavetable playback as resampling, distinguish aliasing from imaging and show tradeoffs between integration order, interpolation and quantisation. This is evidence for a careful wavetable experiment when richer periodic spectra or lower oscillator cost are needed, not permission to switch a lookup table blindly into the present renderer. [DAFx-12 paper](https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/2012/papers/dafx12_submission_69.pdf), [authors' companion page](https://research.spa.aalto.fi/publications/papers/dafx12-hoiws/).

Modal representation exposes resonances and damping independently; digital waveguides instead represent travelling-wave propagation and interaction. Both can give musically coherent body response, but their physical interpretation requires justified excitation and observation. The present decaying additive `bell` is inspired by independent mode behaviour; it is not a physical bell, syrinx, or measured modal model. [Modal representation](https://www.dsprelated.com/freebooks/pasp/Modal_Representation.html), [digital waveguides](https://www.dsprelated.com/freebooks/pasp/Digital_Waveguides.html).

Formant models separate excitation from resonances. Smith discusses cascade/parallel sections and FOF, including difficulty estimating formants when pitch is high and harmonics sparsely sample the envelope. Klatt's original paper distinguishes configurations and voiced/noisy sources; its human-speech examples cannot be assumed to describe bird anatomy or the requested taxa. These sources motivate independently authored resonant colour as a musical design. They do not justify importing their software, speech parameter tables or coefficients. [Smith formant section](https://www.dsprelated.com/freebooks/pasp/Formant_Synthesis_Models.html), [Klatt 1980 paper, university-hosted](https://www.fon.hum.uva.nl/david/ma_ssp/doc/Klatt-1980-JAS000971.pdf).

Truax's original granular work explicitly includes oscillator grains and FM grains, so granular synthesis need not use recordings at all. Its challenge includes both many small events and enough control bandwidth to connect them to larger gestures. Roads' authored micro-time discussion separates sound objects, phrases and finer temporal structures. These ideas suggest an efficient synthetic-grain experiment for chatter/cloud behaviours; no historical hardware capacity transfers into a current CPU claim. [Truax 1988 original paper](https://www.sfu.ca/~truax/Truax_CMJ%20Granular.pdf), [Roads 1998 authored talk abstract](https://compmus.ime.usp.br/sbcm/1998/talks/Roads.html).

A biomimetic syrinx study demonstrates how a physical substrate and relatively simple control can generate different oscillatory regimes. Its apparatus and minimal model are informative competing research, not a demonstrated universal synthesizer for all the requested birds. No physical constants, solver or sound from this paper is used here. [Mukherjee, Mandre and Mahadevan 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5582117/).

The parameter-mapping literature separates data preparation, synthesis parameters and perceptual mappings. For this product that suggests preserving species/individual/source context separately from musical expression; species timbre cannot substitute for genuine event provenance. Timbre can convey a pattern without making every underlying parameter perceptually invertible. [Grond and Berger, Parameter Mapping Sonification](https://sonification.de/handbook/chapters/chapter15/).

## Chosen candidate, rejected assumptions and next experiments

Immediate candidate: fixed eight-partial additive bank, explicit harmonic/inharmonic spectra, independently decaying bell components, seed-controlled onset phases for diffuse multisine, smooth amplitude pulse, amplitude roughness and continuous glide. It reuses one bounded core across the six currently exposed character values. It preserves the old struck PCM at neutral new controls. The character names describe experimental starting spectra, not six complete synthesis engines or a permanent ceiling.

No source requires exactly eight partials, these ratios, this pulse shape or these six labels; those are original bounded experiment choices. No listening superiority is claimed. Do not introduce a generic modular graph, unbounded grain engine, neural dependency or physical solver merely because a survey contains it. Conversely, a failed musical comparison should be allowed to replace the baseline or add an evidenced primitive.

Next discriminating experiments, in priority order:

1. For coarse/airy voices, compare sparse multisine against independently designed filtered seeded noise plus a tonal core. Match level and crest reporting, inspect filter energy/output bounds, then judge texture and fatigue.
2. For related ringing species and voice-like coarse interruptions, compare additive spectral motion with a two-operator FM/PM candidate and a moving-formant source-filter candidate. Use high notes, sustained low notes, mixed articulation and bandlimited high-rate references before judging aliasing.
3. For material-like sounds, compare decaying additive modes with an excited modal bank; audition whether excitation-dependent articulation improves the intended musical role enough to justify state/control complexity.
4. For repeated chatter/clouds, compare smooth AM with fixed-pool synthetic grains and small authored phrase cells. Rate, intra-phrase pitch and colour switching may matter more than a new oscillator. Avoid random call-like ornament on every detection.
5. If oscillator CPU is a demonstrated limit, compare a safely bandlimited wavetable representation using original tables, preserving spectral/gesture behaviour. Measure before choosing optimisation.
6. Treat spectral analysis as offline source research unless a live-analysis product need appears. If live windows are introduced, publish observation/hop/latency rather than hiding buffering.

Audition common-register/common-loudness isolated notes first, followed by proposed defaults, related-family pairs, unrelated broad animal classes and dense mixtures. Mask character labels during preference comparisons. Ablate timbre, register, phrase, roughness and contour independently. Record owner comments and failed examples; numeric waveform differences alone are not evidence of a successful musical representation.
